Daniel fell off a ladder at home in an accident. The doctor says he fractured his hip, or possibly his tibia - they're not sure yet. It's a closed fracture. What would his accident cover pay?

DearPolicy

The Group Insurance Product Summary (page 13) pays the Simple Fracture as a percentage of the Simple Fracture Benefit Limit of S$5,000 for each listed case: Neck, Skull or Spine (Full Break) 100%; Hip 75%; Jaw, Pelvis, Leg, Ankle or Knee 50%; Cheekbone, Shoulder or Hairline Fracture of Skull or Spine 30%; Arm, Elbow, Wrist or Ribs 25%. The page also prints footnotes for these cases: for “Jaw, Pelvis, Leg, Ankle or Knee”: “For the avoidance of doubt, any fractures of toe and foot are not covered” (marked ^); for “Arm, Elbow, Wrist or Ribs”: “For the avoidance of doubt, any fractures of thumb, finger and palm are not covered” (marked #). The Group Personal Accident Benefit Summary (page 8) pays the Simple Fracture or Other Fracture due to Accident as a percentage of Daniel's own sum insured (S$600,000 on the certificate) for each listed case: Neck, Skull or Spine (Full Break) 100%; Hip 75%; Jaw, Pelvis, Leg, Ankle or Knee (Other Fracture) 50%; Cheekbone, Shoulder or Hairline Fracture of Skull or Spine 30%; Arm, Elbow, Wrist or Ribs (Other Fracture) 20%. Under the table it prints: “This Benefit can only be claimed either under the Core Scheme or Voluntary Scheme”. Daniel's documents give two different answers for the same kind of event, and what they say does not settle which applies: these are alternatives from separate documents, never a total. The Group Insurance Product Summary (page 13) takes its percentage of a fixed Simple Fracture Benefit Limit; the Group Personal Accident Benefit Summary (page 8) takes its percentage of Daniel's own sum insured. Neither document says whether one amends, replaces or adds to the other.
