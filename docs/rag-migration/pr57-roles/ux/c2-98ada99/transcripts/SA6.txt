Daniel fractured his tibia in an accident. It's a closed fracture. What would his accident cover pay?

DearPolicy

Daniel's documents give two different answers for this event, and what they say does not settle which applies: these are alternatives from separate documents, never a total.

If the Simple Fracture benefit applies to Daniel's case “Jaw, Pelvis, Leg, Ankle or Knee”, the conditional illustration is S$2,500: 50% of the Simple Fracture benefit limit of S$5,000, in the Group Insurance Product Summary (page 13); it is an illustration from the document's terms, not a payment or an insurer's decision; the page prints a footnote for that row: “For the avoidance of doubt, any fractures of toe and foot are not covered” (marked ^).

If the Simple Fracture or Other Fracture due to Accident benefit applies to Daniel's case “Jaw, Pelvis, Leg, Ankle or Knee” (the row the document labels “Other Fracture”; these documents print no definition of which fractures that is), the conditional illustration is S$300,000: 50% of Daniel's sum insured of S$600,000 (Group Personal Accident, printed as of 1 April 2024, not confirmation of cover on a later date), in the Group Personal Accident Benefit Summary (page 8); it is an illustration from the document's terms, not a payment or an insurer's decision; under the table it prints: “This Benefit can only be claimed either under the Core Scheme or Voluntary Scheme”.

The Group Insurance Product Summary (page 13) takes its percentage of a fixed Simple Fracture Benefit Limit; the Group Personal Accident Benefit Summary (page 8) takes its percentage of Daniel's own sum insured. Neither document says whether one amends, replaces or adds to the other.
