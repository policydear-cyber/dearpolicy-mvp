DearPolicy 1.0.0-rc.14 - how a claim amount is decided

WHAT TO DO AFTER THIS UPDATE

   Nothing. It is already live. No re-read, no re-upload, no command.

   The change is in how DearPolicy decides which benefits respond and how
   they add up, and it applies to the benefit rules already in your database.

WHAT CHANGED, IN ONE BREATH

   - A benefit is matched on the EVENT it is written for, not on words it
     shares with your story. A critical illness product is no longer offered
     for a death, and dengue no longer reaches a critical illness benefit.
   - A critical illness benefit is a living benefit. Once a death is reported
     the death benefits are the claim; an illness diagnosed before the death
     is offered as a separate claim rather than added to it.
   - Benefits are no longer simply added up. Two benefits paid by the SAME
     cover for the SAME event are alternatives: the figure from your own
     certificate is kept and counted, the other stays on its card and says
     which benefit is paid instead of it. Separate covers still stack.
   - An injury scale is not a menu. The benefit that pays is the one whose
     injury actually happened, not every fixed amount in the table.
   - When the amount cannot be calculated yet, the RULE is shown - "S$30.00
     per day after the first 2 days, up to 30 days" - labelled as the rule
     rather than as a payout, and never added to a total.
   - Every card says which cover the figure is paid under and the policy
     number the insurer settles against.
   - Confidence is now read from the evidence: strong only when every counted
     figure came from your own schedule and nothing is still needed.
   - The claim pack states the estimated claim amount itself, struck once.
   - Your own plan's schedule of defined conditions beats the national list
     wherever its benefit table has been read.

   The death claim that returned S$3,600,000 across four benefits returns
   S$1,600,000: S$1,000,000 of group term life plus S$600,000 of group
   personal accident.

HOW THIS IS CHECKED

   scripts/claim-answer-eval.ts runs nine real household scenarios end to end
   with no model involved and asserts which benefits respond, which do not,
   and what the total is. It runs in CI on every push, alongside the type
   check, both regression suites, extraction recall and the production build.

KNOWN LIMITS

   - Waiting periods, survival periods, territorial and age limits are shown
     as conditions on the card rather than evaluated as rules.
   - An accelerated rider that reduces another benefit is not yet modelled;
     both are shown.
   - Where a plan's benefit table has not been read, its list of defined
     critical illnesses is the national standard list, which is a floor. The
     card says so.
   - Insurer claim forms are still not filled in (no form to test against).


DearPolicy 0.8.2 — A Failure You Can Read

ABOUT THAT BLANK DASHBOARD

The dashboard was crashing before it could show you the error. It asked for
data.coverages.filter(...) and the safety check only covered "data", not
"coverages" - so when the API returned an error instead of your portfolio, the
page threw and you got a white screen instead of the reason.

Now you will see the actual message. Run this first when anything 500s:

   npm run db:check

It tells you whether PostgreSQL is running, whether .env.local points at it,
and whether every table is present - then names the command that fixes it.
Nine times out of ten a 500 here means Docker Desktop is not running:

   1. Open Docker Desktop, wait until it says running
   2. npm run db:start
   3. npm run db:status     (wait for healthy)
   4. npm run db:check

AND NOW THE ROWS ACTUALLY TURN INTO AMOUNTS

Reading the page as a table was half the job. The payout rule still had to get
past patterns written for sentences - which is why a cell reading

   25% of S$5,000

was thrown away, for not saying "of the Sum Insured".

Those cells are now read directly:

   25% of S$5,000            ->  S$1,250   (both halves are on the page)
   100% of the Sum Insured   ->  100% of your schedule amount
   Up to S$500 per incident  ->  reimbursement capped at S$500
   S$30 per day              ->  daily rate, cap taken from the same row
   As charged / Nil / ranges ->  left for review, never guessed

A page whose schedule reads cleanly skips the AI pass altogether, so indexing is
quicker too.

THE REASON EVERYTHING SAID "NEEDS CHECKING"

DearPolicy was reading every PDF by gluing all the words together with spaces.
That throws away where each word sits on the page. A benefit table like

   Simple fracture - wrist        25% of S$5,000
   Ambulance fee                  Up to S$500 per incident

became one long run-on sentence, so nothing could work out which amount
belonged to which benefit. That is why you kept getting benefits with no
figures.

It now keeps the position of every word, finds the columns, and reads the page
as rows and cells. On your CareShield booklet this recovered the payout table
that was previously invisible:

   2026 -> $689   2027 -> $717   2028 -> $745   2029 -> $775   2030 -> $806

Before: 27 amounts found, none attached to anything. After: 5 clean rows.

TO PICK THIS UP: click Re-analyse on a document, or run

   npm run policies:index

YOU WERE RIGHT

"A brochure never proves you own S$600,000" is a rule about the AMOUNT.
DearPolicy was applying it to the POLICY, so uploading your own CareShield
welcome booklet got you nothing. People do not collect other insurers'
booklets - uploading one says you hold that cover.

Now, on the Policies page, any document with no named policyholder asks:

   "Whose policy is this?"   [ Desmond ] [ Charmaine ] ...

Click a name and its benefits are used in answers from then on. A figure the
document itself prints - "S$600 each month" - is shown as that person's, marked
"Amount printed in the policy document - not yet confirmed against your own
schedule", and kept out of the headline total until a schedule confirms it.

The one thing a claim still cannot do is turn "100% of the Sum Insured" into a
number, because that sum is on a schedule nobody has uploaded yet. There it says
so plainly and asks you for it.

WHAT 0.6.3 FIXED (your CareShield booklet)

R. "Cannot perform Construct on a detached ArrayBuffer". PDF.js takes ownership
   of the file bytes it is given and leaves nothing behind. DearPolicy reads a
   PDF twice - once for the text, then again to render picture pages for OCR -
   so any PDF containing images failed on the second pass. Your booklet has 3
   image pages out of 7, which is exactly why it stopped. PDF.js now gets its
   own copy.

   Click Re-analyse on that document and it should go through.

S. One booklet often describes several products. Benefit rules were being filed
   under the document's single product name, so a rule from one product could be
   attached to another product's sum insured - which is how an accident rule got
   quoted against a term-life death benefit in your chat. Each page's product
   section is now tracked and benefits are filed under the right one.

INDEXING IS NOW PART OF UPLOADING

You no longer run a command. When you upload a policy, DearPolicy reads its
benefit rules as part of processing it and shows you the progress:

   "Reading the benefit rules - page 12 of 40..."

It is the slow part, and it happens once, while you already expect to wait.
After that every question about that policy is answered from the stored rules.

Reprocessing a document rebuilds its rules. If this step fails, nothing is
broken - the document still works the old way.

FOR THE DOCUMENTS YOU ALREADY UPLOADED, run this once:

   npm run db:setup
   npm run policies:index

New uploads from now on need neither.

IT STILL FEELS LIKE A CHAT

Answering from a stored table does not mean answering like one. Every reply now
ends with a short AI narration that reflects back what you said happened, leads
with the figure, and explains in plain words what could still change it. Where
one missing fact is all that stands between a benefit and a real number, it asks
you for that fact instead of handing you a verdict.

The division of labour is the whole point: the code decides what is covered and
what it pays, the AI only chooses the words - and it still cannot invent a
number, drop a total, promise approval or claim something is not covered. Ask
the same question twice and the amounts are identical; the wording will differ,
the way a person would phrase it differently.

THE ANSWER TO "DO I REALLY NEED AN AI?"

For reading your policy documents: yes. Every insurer writes differently and
that is what a language model is genuinely good at.

For answering your questions: no, and that was the mistake. DearPolicy was
re-reading the raw policy text with the model EVERY time you asked something.
That is why answers took minutes and why the same question could give different
results.

A policy is a contract with fixed rules. "Simple Fracture pays 25% of the
S$5,000 limit, page 14" does not change between questions. So it is now read
once and stored, and questions are answered from that table by ordinary code.

FOR DOCUMENTS ALREADY UPLOADED, run this once:

   npm run db:setup
   npm run policies:index

New uploads index themselves during processing, so this is a one-time backfill.

AFTER THAT:

 - Questions answer in about a second, with no model reading involved.
 - The same question gives the same answer every time.
 - Amounts are calculated by DearPolicy from your schedule and the quoted
   clause, with the page number shown.
 - A broken ankle can no longer surface a death or disability benefit: what
   triggers a benefit is decided by rules you can read, not by the model.

If you have not run the indexer, questions still work the old (slow) way, so
nothing breaks in the meantime.

WHAT 0.5.4 FIXED (from your fall-down-the-stairs test)

O. The chat printed a raw validation error at you. That is fixed: a section that
   cannot be read now says so in plain English, and no model or validation
   output is ever shown.

P. Those two failed sections were not really failures. Qwen had returned one
   field in the wrong form (a sentence where DearPolicy expected a list), and the
   whole section was thrown away and re-read at double the budget - which is
   where much of the five minutes went. The reply is now repaired first, keeping
   whatever is valid and dropping only what is not. A wrong shape is no longer
   retried, because more room cannot fix it.

Q. Duplicate benefit cards are collapsed, and the model is now asked to use the
   benefit name the policy actually prints (Simple Fracture, Ambulance Fee)
   instead of a generic category.

Expect this question to come back faster, with fewer and better-named cards.

WHAT 0.5.3 FIXED (the 24-seconds-per-section problem)

N. Your benchmark settled it. Generation ran at 49 tokens/sec in every case; the
   only difference was how much the model wrote — 105 tokens for a simple schema
   (2.1s) versus 1198 for the real one (24.5s). Time = tokens written.

   Most of those tokens were verbatim policy quotes. DearPolicy only needs a
   quote long enough to find the clause in the page, so quotes are now capped at
   260 characters instead of 900, with fewer quotes per benefit and tighter
   limits everywhere else. Expect roughly a third of the output, and roughly a
   third of the time.

   The two knobs, in .env.local:

      RAG_READING_OUTPUT_TOKENS=900    (lower = faster, try 600)
      RAG_MAX_EVIDENCE_WINDOWS=8       (lower = faster, try 4)

   My 0.5.2 theory about schema bounds was wrong — 24.5s vs 24.7s. It has been
   removed rather than left in the code.

WHAT WAS TRIED AND REMOVED IN 0.5.2

M. Your benchmark showed the GPU was fine: 48 tokens/sec, model fully in VRAM,
   0.8s for a call with a simple output schema. The problem was the schema
   DearPolicy sends. Ollama turns it into a grammar that constrains every token,
   and length limits like "quote: max 900 characters" and the integer bounds get
   expanded into huge rules. That grammar, not the model, was the 2 minutes.

   The schema sent to the model now carries only the SHAPE. Every reply is still
   checked against the full schema afterwards, so nothing looser is accepted.

   Run the benchmark again — it now times the real schema with and without those
   bounds, so you can see the difference directly.

WHAT 0.5.1 CHANGED (what happens with 10 policies)

J. Model calls now go to the contracts retrieval actually matched, in relevance
   order, instead of being split evenly. With ten policies, each candidate gets
   its best section read first, then spare budget deepens the strongest match.
   Contracts with no retrieved evidence cost nothing, and any contract left
   unopened is named in the answer.

K. Cheaper calls: LLM_NUM_BATCH default raised from 64 to 256, and output per
   section capped by RAG_READING_OUTPUT_TOKENS (default 1400).

L. NEW, run this first:

      npm run model:benchmark

   It times one real reading call and tells you how many tokens/sec you get,
   whether the 9B model actually fits in the RTX 5060's 8 GB or is spilling to
   the CPU, and how long a question will take. If it reports that the model is
   partly on the CPU, that is the whole story — a smaller model (qwen3:8b) will
   do more for speed than any setting.

WHAT 0.5.0 FIXED (the 14-minute answer)

H. DearPolicy was unloading the 9B model from the GPU after EVERY evidence
   section and loading it again for the next one. Reading ten sections meant ten
   full model loads, which is where the minutes went. The model now stays loaded
   for the whole analysis and is released once at the end.

I. A question can no longer run without a ceiling. Reading stops after 12
   evidence sections or 5 minutes, whichever comes first, and the answer says
   plainly that sections were left unread. Tune in .env.local:

      RAG_MAX_EVIDENCE_WINDOWS=12
      RAG_ANALYSIS_BUDGET_MS=300000
      RAG_MAX_PASSAGES_PER_DOCUMENT=16
      LLM_KEEP_ALIVE=5m

   For faster answers while testing, try RAG_MAX_EVIDENCE_WINDOWS=6.

WHAT 0.4.9 FIXED

F. The "Model returned invalid structured JSON" error. The model was running out
   of output room part-way through reading a long, detailed message, because
   every fact repeated a 36-character id. Ids are now short handles, the budget
   is doubled, cut-off output is salvaged, and a genuine shortfall is retried
   once. You should no longer see raw JSON in the chat.

G. The chat now shows a proper thinking indicator while analysis runs: animated
   dots, the current stage, a progress bar, contracts read and elapsed time.

WHAT 0.4.8 ADDED

A. DearPolicy was reading only six passages per document. On a 40-page booklet
   that meant the benefit table proving the payout was often never read, which
   is why an answer could come back with no claim amount at all. The budget now
   scales with the size of the document (up to 24 passages). Long documents will
   take noticeably longer to analyse; that is deliberate.

B. Evidence batches the local model failed to finish were being counted as text
   that had been read. They are not any more, and the reported cause is shown.

C. When an amount is calculated, the answer now leads with it and states the
   specific reasons the settled figure could differ — the outstanding policy
   definition, an unconfirmed fact, unverified active status, a reimbursement
   held separately, or unread sections.

D. If DearPolicy needs something, it now asks. A documentary condition becomes
   the question, and a contract with no matching wording prompts you to upload
   the policy wording or benefit summary.

E. New: npm run policies:report
   Prints which documents belong to which family member, which wording is bound
   to each contract and why, and why any wording was NOT bound. Read-only.

If analysis feels too slow on the RTX 5060, lower the ceiling in .env.local:

   RAG_MAX_PASSAGES_PER_DOCUMENT=12

WHAT 0.4.7 ADDED

1. Every answer now shows how much of your policy text was actually read:
   "Complete policy review" or "Partial policy review", with the exact page
   and passage counts per contract. DearPolicy can only say something is
   "not covered" when every processed page was read. Otherwise it says the
   review is incomplete — an unread section is never treated as proof that
   the event is uninsured.

2. Each contract now shows "Active status evidenced" or "Active status not
   recently verified", based on premium receipts and renewal notices matched
   to that same contract, insurer and insured person. This never changes
   ownership or a confirmed insured amount: a missing receipt cannot erase
   cover you have proved with a certificate or schedule.

What to try:

- Ask a natural multi-fact question rather than following a script.
- Say that one fact is not yet known, then supply it later.
- Ask about an independent benefit while another fact remains unknown.
- Ask about people naturally: “Charmaine caught dengue”, “my wife’s flight was delayed” and then “what can she claim?” No profile selector is required.
- Ask “What policies does my family have?” to review a mixed household portfolio.
- Open Previous chats, delete one chat, then test Clear all chat history.
- Report a death in ordinary language, including homicide wording and a location. DearPolicy should retain those facts without assuming the death was accidental.
- Try unrelated scenarios such as a flight delay, vehicle damage, theft, illness or hospital admission. DearPolicy should classify the event, inspect each owned contract and show policy-specific results.
- Where uploaded wording proves the formula, confirm that each card says Potential payout or Potential reimbursement. Additive lump sums should also appear in the potential total at the top.
- Look for the review banner above "Policy review". On a long scanned booklet it should say Partial policy review with the pages read, and the answer should never say the event is not covered.
- Open Policy review and check the new "Evidence read" and "Active status" columns.
- Ask "What policies does my family have?" and check the new Active status column. With no premium receipt uploaded it should read "Active status not recently verified" while the confirmed cover amount stays exactly as before.
- Upload a recent premium receipt or renewal notice for one policy, then ask again. Only that contract should change to "Active status evidenced".

Chat deletion removes conversation/history data only. Uploaded policy documents and verified coverage records remain intact.

Important:

- Keep Ollama running and retain your existing .env.local settings for Qwen, BGE-M3, PostgreSQL and local document storage. Qwen3.5 9B is recommended, but OCR also works while you are still using Qwen3 8B.
- Analysis is intentionally not instant when documents must be read. The progress display reports actual stages and contracts reviewed.
- Long legacy evidence chunks are split into bounded reading windows; they are no longer skipped merely because one stored chunk is oversized.
- DearPolicy does not rely on one universal insurance manual and does not claim it has learned every policy 100%. It uses a broad event classifier to find candidate clauses, then the uploaded contract remains the authority for eligibility and payout.
- DearPolicy uses the PDF's original text whenever it is reliable. PaddleOCR-VL automatically reads only image-backed pages with missing text. Qwen and BGE-M3 are unloaded before OCR and reload automatically when needed, preserving the 8 GB GPU safeguards.
